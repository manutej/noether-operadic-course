Module 1 · Module 1 · Operads

Operads as Compositional Algebra

Many-in, one-out, composed by substitution

Objective: Define an operad, draw a composition tree, and explain algebras over an operad with a concrete example.

Operads organize multi-input operations and their substitution. Classic sources: May, Boardman–Vogt, Loday–Vallette, Markl.

Operads are the algebra of multi-input operations composed by substitution. They are the mathematical home for tree-shaped composition — and therefore for question trees.

Many-in, one-out

An ordinary category composes one-in-one-out arrows. An operad composes n-ary operations: several inputs, one output. The fundamental move is substitution: plug the output of one operation into an input slot of another.

Combinatorially this yields trees. Algebraically it yields types of algebras: associative algebras (As), commutative algebras (Com), Lie algebras (Lie), and many more — including, as we will see, question-answering models.

Definition sketch

A (symmetric) operad O in vector spaces (or sets, or spaces) assigns to each n ≥ 0 a space O(n) of n-ary operations, with:

(1) Composition maps O(k) ⊗ O(n₁) ⊗ ⋯ ⊗ O(nₖ) → O(n₁+⋯+nₖ) implementing substitution.

(2) Symmetric group actions O(n) ↷ Σₙ permuting inputs.

(3) A unit 1 ∈ O(1) for the identity operation.

An O-algebra is a space A with structure maps O(n) ⊗ A⊗ⁿ → A compatible with composition and symmetries.

Why topology cared — and why AI might

May’s little n-cubes operads classify n-fold loop spaces. Boardman–Vogt and later homotopy theorists used operads to encode coherent higher homotopies.

In applied and AI settings, the same tree-shaped substitution language describes wiring of systems (Spivak), design of complex systems (Foley et al.), and — crucially — recursive question decomposition (Bottman–Richardson 2026).

Key terms

  • Arity — Number of inputs of an operation (n for O(n)).
  • Substitution — Plugging outputs into input slots — operadic composition.
  • O-algebra — Object equipped with O-operations satisfying the operad laws.

Module 2 · Module 2 · Questions Operad

The Questions Operad

Question templates as operations

Objective: State the definition of the questions operad, interpret QA models as Q-algebras, and relate tree decomposition to operadic composition.

arXiv:2606.13634 defines Q; arXiv:2606.13649 measures operadic consistency as collapse fidelity across partial decompositions.

Bottman & Richardson (2026) define the questions operad Q: operations are question templates; composition is substitution of sub-answers. QA models are Q-algebras. Consistency is measurable.

From prompting folklore to algebra

Question decomposition — breaking a hard query into sub-queries and composing answers — is ubiquitous in multi-hop QA and agentic systems. Until 2026 it lacked a compact mathematical home.

The questions operad Q takes question templates as operations. Composition corresponds to filling a template’s slots with answers obtained from sub-questions — exactly tree-shaped substitution.

QA models as algebras

A question-answering model becomes an algebra over Q when it supplies, for each template, a map from sub-answers to a parent answer. Different models are different algebras; the operad is shared structure.

This separation matters: evaluation can probe the algebra (the model) against laws that live in the operad (the templates and composition).

Operadic consistency

The companion paper (Bottman–Liu–Richardson 2026) defines operadic consistency: the model’s direct answer to a compositional query should agree with the answer obtained by composing a stated decomposition.

Empirically, this label-free signal correlates strongly with accuracy on multi-hop benchmarks and can outperform temperature-based self-consistency baselines.

In our research method, operadic consistency is the compositionality test for verbal, non-deterministic domains.

Relation to Tree of Thoughts

Yao et al. (2023) Tree of Thoughts explores multiple reasoning paths with search. ToT is a policy over trees; Q is an algebraic type for trees of questions. They are complementary: ToT can be read as search in a free (or free-ish) algebra for Q-like structure, while operadic consistency supplies an invariant the search should respect.

Key terms

  • Question template — An n-ary operation in Q specifying how sub-answers form a parent answer.
  • Operadic consistency — Direct answer ≈ composed answer from a decomposition tree.

Module 3 · Module 3 · Double Operads

Double Operadic Systems

Interfaces, interactions, open systems

Objective: Explain double operads for open systems and how interfaces compose with interactions in a systems-theoretic setting.

arXiv:2505.18329 packages open systems as modules over a double category of interfaces — the double algebraic theory for compositional systems.

Libkind & Myers (2025) present a double operadic theory of systems: open systems as modules over a double category of interfaces and interactions — compositional systems theory with two axes of composition.

Why double?

Ordinary operads compose operations along trees. Systems engineering also needs a horizontal story: interfaces that connect, resources that flow, maps between systems.

Double operads (and the related double-categorical packages in DOTS) let interactions act on several independent component systems in parallel, returning a composite system — critical for systems-of-systems.

Open systems as modules

DOTS packages a collection of open systems, their interactions, and their maps into a symmetric monoidal loose right module of systems over a symmetric monoidal double category of interfaces and interactions.

Examples in the paper include open Petri nets and other applied compositional systems — connecting to Spivak-style wiring diagrams and Foley et al.’s operads for design.

Link to question research

Knowledge domains and agentic pipelines can be read as open systems with typed interfaces (schemas, tools, retrieval ports). Double operadic composition is a candidate algebra for ‘research systems’ that compose domain modules with interaction protocols (search, collapse, consistency check).

This course does not claim a finished double-operadic model of research agents; it situates DOTS as the existing formal systems theory closest to that ambition.

Key terms

  • Open system — A system with typed interfaces to an environment or other systems.
  • Double category — Category-like structure with two composable directions (e.g. horizontal/vertical).

Module 4 · Module 4 · Noether

Noether’s Theorem & Conserved Quantities

Symmetry implies conservation

Objective: State Noether’s theorem, explain conserved quantities from generators (Baez), and locate Noether currents in factorization algebras.

Baez arXiv:2006.14741; Costello–Gwilliam factorization algebras; Gwilliam–Williams higher symmetries arXiv:1810.06534.

Symmetry implies conservation. From Emmy Noether’s 1918 theorems through Baez’s foundational analysis to factorization-algebraic Noether currents — the modern toolkit for invariants.

The classical statement

Noether’s first theorem: every continuous symmetry of the action of a physical system (with appropriate regularity) corresponds to a conservation law. Time translation ↔ energy; spatial translation ↔ momentum; gauge symmetry ↔ charge.

The conserved quantity is often called a Noether charge; the associated flow is a Noether current.

Getting to the bottom (Baez)

Baez (2020) examines the assumptions behind the symmetry–conservation link in classical and quantum settings. A key pattern: map observables to generators so each observable generates a one-parameter group that preserves itself — then conservation follows.

This clarification is useful outside physics: it tells us what structure we must exhibit on a QA-graph before we may honestly speak of ‘Noether charges of insight’.

Factorization algebras

Costello–Gwilliam develop factorization algebras as local-to-global observables of field theories. Within this language, symmetries act and Noether theorems can be formulated beyond purely perturbative regimes.

Gwilliam–Williams (2018) construct higher Kac–Moody-type algebras encoding symmetries of holomorphic field theories — a model of ‘higher’ conserved structure.

Key terms

  • Noether current — Local flow whose integral (charge) is conserved by a symmetry.
  • Factorization algebra — Local-to-global assignment of observables with disjoint-union multiplications.

Module 5 · Module 5 · Lie & Graphs

Lie Operads & Graph Complexes

Brackets on edges, Jacobi on flows

Objective: Define the Lie operad, state the Jacobi identity, and explain how graph complexes model infinitesimal symmetries on graphs.

Trimble notes; Willwacher arXiv:1009.1654; Kimura–Stasheff–Voronov hep-th/9307114; Kapranov alg-geom/9704009.

The Lie operad encodes brackets and Jacobi. Graph complexes give geometric models for related Lie structures — a language for infinitesimal symmetries on graphs, including candidate QA-graphs.

Lie operad

Algebras over the Lie operad are Lie algebras: a bilinear bracket that is skew-symmetric and satisfies the Jacobi identity.

Trimble’s notes present the operad combinatorially; Ferraioli’s gentle introduction situates Lie among standard algebraic operads.

Jacobi as coherence of twists

Jacobi is not optional decoration: it is the coherence law for triple brackets. In the Lego-castle metaphor, multi-twists of symmetries must cycle without warping the build.

Any proposed bracket on edge transformations of a question tree must satisfy Jacobi to be a Lie structure.

Graph complexes

Kontsevich’s graph complex organizes graphs with a differential related to edge operations. Willwacher (2010) proved that its degree-0 cohomology is the Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra grt₁ — a deep link between graphs and Lie theory.

Kimura–Stasheff–Voronov related operads of moduli spaces to homotopy Lie algebras in string theory. Kapranov’s work connects Atiyah classes and Lie-algebraic invariants.

For this course: graph complexes are the best existing formal home for ‘Lie brackets on edges of a graph’ when the graph is combinatorial rather than a smooth manifold.

Key terms

  • Lie bracket — Skew-symmetric bilinear operation [−,−] with Jacobi.
  • Graph complex — Chain complex of graphs encoding combinatorial Lie/deformation data.

QA-Graphs & Noether on Question Trees

Symmetries of the answer space

Objective: Model a question tree as a graph, identify candidate symmetries, and propose conserved insight cores under operadic substitution.

Combines Bottman consistency, graph-complex Lie structure, and factorization-algebra Noether currents on the QA-graph.

Assemble the pieces: question trees as graphs, Lie language for edge symmetries, operadic consistency as collapse fidelity, conserved insight cores as candidate Noether charges.

The QA-graph

Nodes: questions and answers (or question templates and answer values). Edges: ‘is sub-question of’, ‘answers’, ‘substitutes into’. The composition tree of a decomposition is a subgraph.

Symmetries of interest include: permuting independent sibling sub-questions; replacing a sub-tree by another with the same compositional type; continuous deformations in embedding space that preserve answer equality up to tolerance.

What already exists

Operadic consistency already measures a form of invariance under partial collapse (Bottman–Liu–Richardson).

Lie/graph-complex theory supplies brackets and Jacobi for graph edges (Willwacher et al.).

Factorization-algebra Noether theorems supply a template for currents from symmetries (Costello–Gwilliam, Gwilliam–Williams).

What is still open (honestly)

A fully rigorous Noether theorem for discrete QA-graphs with LLM-valued algebras is not a standard textbook theorem as of 2026. This module maps a research program, not a closed proof.

Candidate conserved quantities: claims that survive all sibling permutations; facts present in every consistent collapse; structural types (arity signatures) invariant under substitution.

Candidate currents: how ‘insight mass’ flows along edges when a tree is rewritten — still metaphorical until a precise generator map is fixed (Baez’s lesson).

Key terms

  • QA-graph — Graph of questions, answers, and substitution edges.
  • Collapse fidelity — Agreement between direct and composed answers (operadic consistency).

Module 7 · Module 7 · Research Method

Meta-Operadic Research Method

Question trees as a research instrument

Objective: Execute a full meta-operadic loop: decompose, align literature, answer leaves, collapse, extract conserved quantities.

The operadic-question-decomposition protocol as a reproducible research skill, with failure modes (premature category collapse).

Turn the theory into a loop you can run: decompose → align literature → answer leaves → collapse → extract conserved quantities → recurse. Avoid premature category collapse.

The loop

1. Initialization: partition the domain into Level-1 atomic questions that cover without gap or overlap.

2. Recursive decomposition: apply arity-n templates to each node; every child remains a genuine question.

3. Literature alignment: attach full PDFs that help answer that node (not generic topic dumps).

4. Answer & collapse: answer leaves; compose upward; score operadic consistency.

5. Conserved quantities: list claims invariant under the symmetries you can name.

6. Recurse or reconstitute: either deepen a branch or write the synthesis.

Failure modes

Premature category collapse: renaming a question as a solved concept — you can no longer test composition.

Unprovenanced synthesis: merging papers without citation — breaks truth conservation.

Fidelity loss: high inconsistency between direct and composed answers — debug the algebra (model) or the templates.

Broken coherence: substitutions that do not reassociate — fix the operadic structure of your templates.

Adversarial multi-agent use

In the Noether course pipeline, researchers fan out by topic; a single wiki-writer merges with provenance; lesson agents may only read the wiki; a meta-reviewer adversarially checks contradictions and hype.

The same pattern applies to question-tree research: separate agents for decompose / search / answer / collapse-check, with a conservation lens that rejects uncited claims.

Key terms

  • Atomic question — A concrete, answerable question — not a topic label.
  • Premature collapse — Treating a question node as a finished category too early.

Operad

operads

An operad is a device that encodes multi-input operations and the coherent ways they substitute into one another (many-in, one-out composition).

Classically introduced in topology (Boardman–Vogt, May) to describe iterated loop spaces via little cubes, algebraic operads organize types of algebras: associative, commutative, Lie, Gerstenhaber, and their homotopy-coherent versions.
Combinatorially, an operad O consists of spaces O(n) of n-ary operations, with composition maps that substitute outputs of operations into inputs of others, plus equivariant symmetric-group actions (or planar variants).
An algebra over an operad O is a space A with maps O(n) × Aⁿ → A satisfying associativity of substitution — the ‘dictionary’ that turns abstract operations into concrete computations.
Standard references: May’s Geometry of Iterated Loop Spaces; Loday–Vallette Algebraic Operads; Markl’s survey Operads and PROPs; Leinster’s Higher Operads, Higher Categories.

Related: algebra-over-operad, questions-operad, lie-operad, composition-tree

Algebra over an operad

operads

A concrete carrier space equipped with operations specified by an operad, compatible with composition.

If Q is the questions operad, a QA model is an algebra over Q: each question template (operation) is realized by the model’s map from sub-answers to a parent answer.
This re-framing is the central contribution of Bottman–Richardson (2026): multi-step reasoning becomes operadic algebra rather than ad-hoc prompting folklore.

Questions operad Q

operads

The operad whose operations are question templates and whose composition is substitution of sub-answers (Bottman–Richardson, 2026).

Operations in Q correspond to ways of asking a parent question in terms of sub-questions. Composition is the tree-shaped substitution of answers into templates.
QA models become Q-algebras. Operadic consistency asks whether the model’s direct answer agrees with the answer obtained by composing partial collapses of a decomposition tree.
This is the closest formalization of meta-operadic question-tree research skills as of mid-2026.

Noether’s theorem

noether

Continuous symmetries of the action yield conserved quantities (charges/currents). Modern treatments clarify the map from observables to generators.

Emmy Noether’s first theorem (1918) links variational symmetries to conservation laws in classical field theory.
Baez (2020) isolates assumptions: mapping observables to generators so each observable generates a one-parameter group that preserves itself.
In factorization algebras (Costello–Gwilliam), Noether theorems extend beyond perturbative QFT; higher symmetries appear as higher Kac–Moody-type structures (Gwilliam–Williams).

Related: conserved-quantity, factorization-algebra, noether-on-qa

Conserved quantity

noether

An invariant associated to a symmetry; in research trees, an insight core that survives substitution and collapse.

In physics: Noether charge integrating a current. In this course’s metaphor: a claim or structural fact that remains fixed under reordering of independent sub-questions, partial collapses, and literature re-alignment.
Non-degeneracy conditions (Baez) prevent ‘trivial’ conserved quantities that carry no information.

Related: noether-theorem, operadic-consistency

Factorization algebra

noether

Local-to-global structure on manifolds capturing observables of field theories; home for modern Noether theorems.

Factorization algebras assign cochain complexes of observables to opens, with multiplication maps for disjoint unions, and descent properties.
They provide a rigorous setting in which symmetries act and conserved currents are constructed (Costello–Gwilliam program).

Related: noether-theorem, higher-symmetries

Lie operad

operads

The operad whose algebras are Lie algebras; generated by a skew-symmetric binary bracket subject to Jacobi.

The Lie operad encodes [x,y] = −[y,x] and the Jacobi identity [[x,y],z] + cycl = 0.
Trimble’s notes give a careful combinatorial presentation. Graph complexes provide models for related Lie structures on graphs (Kontsevich, Willwacher).

Related: graph-complex, jacobi-identity, operad

Kontsevich graph complex

graphs

A complex of graphs with a differential encoding edge contractions/expansions; models deformations and Lie structures relevant to symmetries on graphs.

Willwacher (2010) showed that the degree-0 cohomology of Kontsevich’s graph complex is the Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra grt₁.
For QA-graphs, graph complexes supply a language for infinitesimal edge transformations that preserve connectivity — candidates for Lie brackets on question-tree edges.

Related: lie-operad, noether-on-qa, jacobi-identity

Jacobi identity

foundations

[[x,y],z] + [[y,z],x] + [[z,x],y] = 0 — coherence of triple brackets in Lie algebras.

In the Lego-castle metaphor used in this research program: Jacobi ensures that multi-twists of castle symmetries cycle without warping the structure.
Any proposed Lie bracket on QA-graph edges must satisfy Jacobi to be a valid Lie operad algebra.

Related: lie-operad, graph-complex

Tree of Thoughts

llm

Yao et al. (2023): deliberate multi-path reasoning over a tree of intermediate thoughts.

ToT generalizes Chain-of-Thought by maintaining multiple reasoning branches and search procedures.
Relative to the questions operad: ToT implements a search policy over trees that could be seen as free (or free-ish) Q-terms, but without the explicit algebra and consistency metric of Bottman et al.

Meta-operadic question decomposition

method

A research method: generate arity-n question trees, align literature, answer leaves, collapse, extract Noether-style conserved insights.

Protocol steps: (1) partition domain into Level-1 atomic questions; (2) recurse with operadic templates; (3) attach full-PDF literature per node; (4) answer and compose; (5) measure operadic consistency; (6) identify conserved quantities.
Primary failure mode: collapsing a question node into a category/concept too early — losing the operational atomicity that makes composition testable.
Implemented as the operadic-question-decomposition skill in this research program.

Noether structure on QA-graphs

Proposed extension: symmetries of question–answer graphs yield conserved insight cores under operadic substitution.

Still an open research direction. Existing ingredients: (i) questions operad + consistency; (ii) Lie/graph-complex language for edge symmetries; (iii) factorization-algebra Noether currents as template.
This course treats the extension carefully: we map what exists, state what is conjectural, and reserve novelty claims for the thesis future-work section.

Failure modes of question-tree research

method

Premature category collapse, fidelity loss under substitution, broken coherence of multi-level composition.

Premature collapse: treating a node as a solved ‘topic’ rather than an open question — blocks compositionality testing.
Fidelity loss: composed answers diverge from direct answers (operadic inconsistency).
Broken Jacobi/associativity: symmetry flows or substitutions that do not reassociate coherently produce non-reproducible ‘insights’.
Unprovenanced synthesis: claims without source URLs — forbidden by the Noether course conservation of truth.

Operadic Question Decomposition, Compositionality Tests, and Noetherian Invariants on Question–Answer Graphs

A Critical Synthesis of Existing Mathematics and LLM Evaluation Practice

§1 Introduction and scope

Compositional reasoning with large language models is widely practiced and weakly formalized. Practitioners decompose hard questions into sub-questions, explore trees of thoughts, and attempt to reassemble answers. Until very recently, this practice lacked a native algebraic vocabulary.

In June 2026, Bottman and Richardson proposed that operads — mathematical structures for many-input operations composed by substitution — are the natural home for question decomposition. Their questions operad Q has operations given by question templates; composition is substitution of sub-answers; QA models are algebras over Q. A companion paper introduces operadic consistency, a label-free agreement measure between direct and composed answers, empirically correlated with multi-hop accuracy.

Independently, compositional systems theory has advanced double operadic packages for open systems (Libkind–Myers, 2025), while mathematical physics continues to refine Noether theorems in factorization-algebraic language (Costello–Gwilliam; Gwilliam–Williams). Graph complexes supply Lie-theoretic control of combinatorial graphs (Willwacher).

This thesis synthesizes these strands as they stand in the published and preprint literature, distinguishes theorems from program sketches, and formulates open problems at their interfaces. Throughout, we adopt a conservation discipline: every factual claim is tied to a citable source; speculative extensions are labeled as such.

§2 Background: operads and algebras

An operad organizes a family of n-ary operations with coherent substitution and (optionally) symmetric group actions. Foundational sources include May’s geometry of iterated loop spaces, Boardman–Vogt’s homotopy-invariant algebraic structures, and the modern algebraic synthesis of Loday–Vallette. Markl’s survey and Leinster’s higher operads treatise extend the landscape to PROPs and higher categories.

An algebra over an operad is a carrier equipped with structure maps realizing those operations. This distinction — operad as type of structure, algebra as instance — is essential when we later separate question templates (operad) from a particular LLM (algebra).

Pedagogically accessible accounts (e.g. Ferraioli 2025) make the same definitions available without full topological prerequisites, which matters for interdisciplinary readers from AI evaluation.

§3 The questions operad and operadic consistency

Bottman–Richardson (arXiv:2606.13634) define the questions operad Q. Operations correspond to question templates; composition is the substitution of sub-answers into parent templates; the resulting trees are the formal counterparts of decomposition trees used in multi-hop QA.

QA models are interpreted as Q-algebras: each template is realized by the model’s map from tuples of sub-answers to a parent answer. This yields a precise sense in which two models share structure (the same operad) while differing as algebras.

Bottman–Liu–Richardson (arXiv:2606.13649) define operadic consistency: agreement between the model’s direct answer to a compositional query and the answer obtained by composing a stated decomposition. Across twelve LLMs and four multi-hop datasets, consistency correlates with accuracy and can outperform temperature-based self-consistency baselines.

We emphasize what these papers do claim (an operadic framework and an empirical metric) and what they do not (a full deformation theory of Q-algebras, or a Noether theorem).

§4 Relation to Tree of Thoughts and agentic decomposition

Yao et al. (2023) introduce Tree of Thoughts (ToT), a framework for deliberate problem solving that maintains multiple reasoning branches and search procedures over intermediate thoughts. ToT is best read as a family of search policies over tree-structured intermediate states.

Relative to Q: ToT does not define an operad, but its trees are candidates for free (or free-ish) terms in a questions-like algebra. Operadic consistency supplies an invariant that any such search ought to approximate: composed answers along a decomposition should match direct answers when the algebra is consistent.

Agentic research skills that recursively expand questions are operationally close to free generation in Q followed by evaluation in a model algebra. The main methodological hazard is premature category collapse — replacing an open question by a topic label — which destroys the ability to test compositionality.

§5 Double operadic systems theory

Libkind–Myers (arXiv:2505.18329) develop a double operadic theory of systems (DOTS). Open systems, their interactions, and maps are packaged as a module over a double category of interfaces and interactions. Parallel and sequential composition coexist.

This is the existing formal apparatus closest to a ‘double algebraic theory for compositional systems’ in the sense of systems-of-systems with typed boundaries. Related applied operadic systems work includes Spivak’s wiring diagrams and Foley et al.’s operads for complex system design.

We record, without overclaiming, that knowledge bases and tool-using agents have natural interface types (schemas, APIs, retrieval ports). A full DOTS model of research pipelines remains future work; the contribution of this section is placement, not construction.

§6 Noether theorems: classical to factorization-algebraic

Noether’s first theorem associates continuous variational symmetries with conservation laws. Baez (arXiv:2006.14741) isolates structural assumptions, notably a map from observables to generators of one-parameter groups that preserve those observables.

In the Costello–Gwilliam factorization algebra program, observables of field theories are organized local-to-global; Noether-type results and higher symmetries (including higher Kac–Moody structures of Gwilliam–Williams, arXiv:1810.06534) are formulated in that language. Recent notes (e.g. arXiv:2504.05626) continue the development of factorization-algebraic Noether theorems.

These sources supply the only rigorous templates we use when speaking of ‘currents’ and ‘charges’ outside classical field theory.

§7 Lie operads and graph complexes

The Lie operad encodes skew-symmetric brackets with Jacobi identity; Trimble’s notes remain a clear combinatorial reference. Graph complexes, going back to Kontsevich and culminating in Willwacher’s identification of H⁰ with the Grothendieck–Teichmüller Lie algebra grt₁ (arXiv:1009.1654), provide Lie structures native to graphs.

Kimura–Stasheff–Voronov (hep-th/9307114) connect moduli operads to homotopy Lie algebras; Kapranov (alg-geom/9704009) develops related Lie-algebraic geometry. Together these form the best existing language for infinitesimal edge transformations on combinatorial graphs.

If question–answer architectures are graphs, this is the natural place to look for candidate brackets — subject to Jacobi and to a Baez-style generator map before any conservation claim.

§8 Towards Noether structure on QA-graphs (program, not theorem)

We outline a research program, explicitly not a theorem: (i) formalize QA-graphs with nodes for questions/answers and edges for substitution; (ii) identify symmetry groupoids (sibling permutation, type-preserving rewrites); (iii) seek Lie-algebraic infinitesimal models via graph-complex techniques; (iv) define candidate charges as predicates invariant under those symmetries; (v) relate charges to operadic consistency as an empirical shadow of invariance under collapse.

Gaps: LLM answer maps are stochastic and non-linear; discrete graphs lack the smooth variational structure of classical Noether; non-degeneracy of charges needs definition; no published construction yet identifies a Noether current for Q-algebras in the literature surveyed here.

What we can already use operationally: operadic consistency as a collapse invariant; sibling-permutation tests for claim stability; citation-backed wiki discipline as a sociological conservation law for research artifacts.

§9 Meta-operadic research method

The method used to explore this space — and recommended for adjacent domains — is recursive question decomposition with literature alignment: Level-1 atomic questions partition the domain; sub-questions expand operadically; each node carries full-PDF sources that help answer it; leaves are answered; answers collapse with consistency checks; conserved claims are extracted; premature category collapse is treated as a first-class failure mode.

This method is compatible with multi-agent orchestration: separate roles for decomposition, retrieval, answering, and adversarial consistency review, with a single-writer wiki substrate for facts — the same conservation pattern used in Noether courseware production.

§10 Related work summary

Operad foundations: May; Boardman–Vogt; Loday–Vallette; Markl; Leinster; Getzler–Jones.

Applied compositional systems: Spivak; Fong–Spivak; Foley et al.; Libkind–Myers.

LLM structured reasoning: Yao et al. ToT; Bottman et al. questions operad and consistency.

Symmetry and conservation: Baez; Costello–Gwilliam; Gwilliam–Williams; classical Noether literature.

Graphs and Lie: Willwacher; Kontsevich lineage; Kimura–Stasheff–Voronov; Kapranov; Conant–Vogtmann.

§11 Future work (potential, not claimed results)

Open problem A. Give a fully precise, checkable definition of a symmetry groupoid of a finite QA-graph induced by a Q-algebra, including stochastic algebras with temperature.

Open problem B. Construct, or prove obstruction to, a Lie bracket on edge derivations of QA-graphs that recovers sibling permutation at the group level and satisfies Jacobi.

Open problem C. Relate operadic consistency scores to approximate conservation of a defined charge under partial collapses; provide concentration bounds.

Open problem D. Build a double-operadic model of tool-using research agents with interfaces for retrieval, citation, and consistency checking, in the DOTS style.

Open problem E. Produce certified educational instruments (as this course attempts) whose fact substrate is machine-checked against source hashes and link liveness.

None of A–E is claimed as solved in this thesis. Their value is as a well-posed frontier extracted from the synthesis.

§12 Conclusion

The research space of operadic question decomposition is no longer informal. The questions operad and operadic consistency provide a native algebra and metric for multi-step verbal reasoning. Double operadic systems theory and classical operad foundations supply the broader compositional context. Noether theorems and graph-complex Lie theory offer the best existing language for symmetries and invariants — but a full Noether theorem for QA-graphs remains an open program.

The disciplined stance of this thesis is deliberate: map what exists, cite it, teach it, and only then list what might exist next. That is both a scholarly ethic and a conservation law.
